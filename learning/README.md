# Learning GHZ communication cost

The learning route starts with a small physical question: two laboratories share Bell pairs with a third laboratory, and the three want one coherent GHZ state. A local measurement gives a correction label. Whether that label is communicated changes the actual output state.

The single educational anchor is **John Watrous, *The Theory of Quantum Information* (2018)**. Use its official author manuscript for the section references. The [Watrous bridge](bridge.md) gives targeted readings, translates notation and fidelity conventions, works through one-bit fusion, and supplies the missing connections to the network proofs.

## The shared understanding map

The [30-milestone map](milestones.md) is defined by the scientific project and is common to every reader. It has two phases:

| Phase | Milestones | Route from local objects to the whole problem |
|---|---|---|
| Background/preliminaries | [M01](milestones.md#m01)–[M12](milestones.md#m12) | Registers and states, instruments and branch probabilities, independent sources, LOCC transcripts, fidelity, packing and entropy, ending with the complete preparation model. |

The longer narrative phase accommodates two converse methods and three distinct communication questions. The map gives central questions, understanding targets, earlier dependencies, conceptual connections and source pointers. Specialist calculations and full proofs remain in the scientific pages.

## Boundaries to keep visible

The initial quantum sources are independent and bipartite conditional on shared classical randomness. The communication model charges every information-bearing choice. Fidelity is squared overlap with the specified pure target, and a collective target uses global block fidelity.

A rare exact branch is compatible with poor unconditional fidelity. A small average cost is compatible with expensive successful executions. A retained hub and a discarded helper define different output tasks. The bridge and milestones keep these distinctions attached to the corresponding theorem rather than treating them as implementation details.

The source readings support the foundations. The project develops the graph packing, Finner application, transcript counting and message-entropy arguments, with primary research attribution in [related work](../references/related-work.md).
